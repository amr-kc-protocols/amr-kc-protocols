How to renew your Kansas EMS license

For EMTs, AEMTs and Paramedics. Plain steps for first-timers.

Short version Dates CE hours Steps Fees Missed it? CE questions Links

The short version

  1. Get your training hours (called CE, continuing education) before your license runs out.
  2. Put every CE certificate into the KBEMS online portal.
  3. Fill out the renewal and pay in the portal between August 1 and December 1 of the year your license runs out.

KBEMS is the Kansas Board of Emergency Medical Services. They issue your license. In Kansas the license is called a certificate, so you will see both words.

Renewing with Kansas is not the same as renewing with the National Registry (NREMT). They are two separate things with their own rules. This guide is only about Kansas.

Know your dates

Your license runs out on December 31.

It lasts until December 31 of the second full year after it was issued.

Example: licensed in June 2025 → the 2 full years are 2026 and 2027 → it runs out December 31, 2027. That means your first license can last a bit more than 2 years.

Not sure? Log in to the portal. Your expiration date is shown there and on your card.

How many CE hours you need

You need these hours once every 2-year cycle. Each level has core hours (set topics) and flex hours (any approved EMS topic).

Watch out: some CE websites say Kansas EMTs need 40 hours and AEMTs need 50. Those are National Registry numbers. Kansas needs 28 (EMT), 44 (AEMT), 60 (Paramedic).

  • Fill every core topic. Extra hours in one topic do not fill a different topic.
  • Extra core hours do count toward flex.
  • You can earn the hours any time in your cycle, even all in one year.
  • You can claim at most 12 hours in one day.

Step by step

  1. Log in to the portal. Go to the KBEMS licensing portal. First time? Create an account. Every Kansas-certified EMS provider is required to have one. (How to create an account, PDF)
  2. Check your expiration date and your CE. The portal shows the CE hours already on file. Some Kansas classes add hours for you, but don't count on it. Compare what's there to the hours table below.
  3. Take the CE you still need. Use approved courses only. See CE questions.
  4. Add any missing hours. Pick the topic, tap the + sign, enter the course and upload the certificate. For ACLS, BLS, PALS and similar classes, upload the CE certificate, not your provider card. See ACLS, BLS, PALS. (KBEMS guide: How to Check and Enter CE, PDF)
  5. Fill out the renewal application in the portal between August 1 and December 1. Kansas does not take paper renewals.
  6. Pay the fee in the portal. See Fees.
  7. Watch your email and the portal. Once approved, you can print your new card from the portal.
  8. Keep your CE certificates for at least 3 years. KBEMS can audit you. See Audits.

Missed the deadline?

Once your license expires, stop working at that level right away. Tell your supervisor. Don't run calls until KBEMS reinstates you.

Expired less than 2 years ago

  • Log in to the portal and fill out the Reinstatement Application for Expired Provider Certification.
  • Pay the reinstatement fee ($120 EMT/AEMT, $200 Paramedic).
  • Show your completed CE.

Expired more than 2 years ago

  • EMT and AEMT: you have to take the whole course again.
  • Paramedic: the rules are different. Call KBEMS at 785-296-7296 before you do anything.

From K.A.R. 109-15-1 and the KBEMS FAQ.

CE questions

What counts as CE?

Training that is approved for EMS credit. A course counts if it is approved by one of these:

  • Kansas (a KBEMS-approved class or provider)
  • CAPCE (see the next question)
  • Another state's EMS office

If none of these approved it, it does not count, unless you get it approved after the fact. See My course wasn't approved.

What is CAPCE?

CAPCE (the Commission on Accreditation for Pre-Hospital Continuing Education) is the national group that approves EMS CE. Most online EMS CE companies are CAPCE-approved.

Rule for online and self-paced courses: Kansas only accepts them if they are CAPCE-approved. The certificate itself must say it is CAPCE-approved. If it doesn't, Kansas won't accept it.

Check before you pay. Look for “CAPCE” and a course or provider number on the course page. You can also look up providers on capce.org.

Can I use hours from another state?

Yes. A course approved by another state's EMS office can count in Kansas.

  1. In the portal, go to the topic and tap +.
  2. Choose KS-781 Other State CE.
  3. Enter the course details and upload the certificate.

The certificate should show which state approved the course. If it doesn't, ask the course provider for proof before you enter it.

CAPCE-approved courses from anywhere in the country count too. They don't need to be from Kansas.

My course wasn't approved. Can I still get credit?

Maybe. Kansas has a retroactive approval process:

  • You must apply within 180 days of finishing the course.
  • Apply in the portal. Paper forms are not accepted.
  • Upload your certificate (or college transcript) and the course objectives.
  • You name a Medical Director who approves the course for credit.
  • If KBEMS finds a problem, you have 15 days to fix it or the request is dropped.

Wait for approval before entering the hours. Once approved, enter them under KS-9999.

Do ACLS, PALS, PHTLS or BLS count? What proof do I upload?

They count only when the class is approved for EMS CE (Kansas, CAPCE or another state), the same as any other course. Many are, but not all. Check the certificate or ask your training officer before you count on it.

Your provider card is not proof of CE.

KBEMS will not accept the ACLS, BLS, PALS (or PHTLS, PEARS, etc.) provider card or eCard. The card only shows you can do the skill. It does not show CE hours or who approved them.

Upload the Continuing Education Certificate for the class instead. It is a separate document from the card.

How to tell you have the right one. The CE certificate:

  • Is titled “Continuing Education Certificate” (or similar), not “Provider” or “eCard”.
  • Shows your name and the course title (for example, “Basic Life Support Classroom Course”).
  • Shows the CE hours (for example, “accredited for 3.25 Basic CEH”).
  • Shows the approval: for American Heart Association classes, a CAPCE provider number and a CAPCE activity number.

Where to get it. The card and the CE certificate don't always come together. If you only got the card, ask your instructor or the Training Center how to claim the CE certificate. Get it right after class, not in November.

Is there a limit on online hours?

We could not find a Kansas limit on online hours in the rules. Every online course still has to be CAPCE-approved.

If you also keep your National Registry card, NREMT has its own limits on self-paced online hours. Those are NREMT rules, not Kansas rules.

Can the same hours count for Kansas and the National Registry?

Often yes, if the course is approved for both. But the two renewals are separate: different hour totals, different deadlines, different websites. Renewing one does not renew the other.

Can I do all my hours at once?

Yes, any time during your 2-year cycle. But you can only claim 12 hours per day. Don't leave it to November. Classes fill up and the December 1 deadline comes fast.

I took a class but the hours aren't in the portal.

Not every class sends hours to the portal for you. Online and out-of-state courses almost never do. Add them yourself with the certificate (Step 4 above). If a Kansas class still isn't showing after a few weeks, ask the class sponsor.

What is a CE audit?

KBEMS can check that your CE hours are real. You might be picked at random, or because of a complaint.

If you are picked, you get a letter asking for proof of your CE.

  • Keep every CE certificate for at least 3 years.
  • Upload certificates to the portal as you go. That is the easiest way to keep them.
  • A good certificate shows: your name, course title, date, hours, provider name, and the approval (CAPCE number or state approval).
  • For ACLS, BLS, PALS and similar classes, keep the CE certificate. The provider card is not proof of CE hours.

Hours you can't prove can be taken away, and that can put your license at risk.

What if my hours are short on December 1?

Finish the hours and renew before December 31. You pay the late fee, but your license doesn't expire. After December 31 you need the reinstatement process. See Missed it?

Who do I call with questions?
