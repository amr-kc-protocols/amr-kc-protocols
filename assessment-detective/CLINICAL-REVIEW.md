# Assessment Detective — clinical review, October 2026

A pre-review of the six draft cases, written to make medical director sign-off quick. It is
not the sign-off: every case stays `draft` until Dr. Deshmukh or Clinical Education approves it.

**What was checked:** every care option, dose and scope level against the AMR KC protocols
(effective 15 May 2026); whether the vitals, findings and responses are physiologically
plausible and consistent with each other; whether the "best" answers match the protocol and
current evidence; and whether the teaching points overstate the evidence.

**Result:** no case teaches anything unsafe. Four errors were fixed (below). Eight
questions went to medical direction; seven are settled and recorded below, one is open.

## Case by case

### 1 · Not Herself — hypoglycemia mimicking stroke
Sound. Hypoglycemic hemiparesis that resolves with glucose is well described, though the
evidence is case series (low quality). The case rightly keeps stroke on the list and makes a
returning deficit a stroke alert. Recurrence on glipizide is well established; excluding
sulfonylurea patients from treat-and-release matches NAEMSP guidance. Doses match VIII-A2,
VIII-A5, III-7 and III-22. Decision 4 confirmed.

### 2 · Can't Catch Her Breath — hypertensive emergency with acute heart failure
Rebuilt on VIII-A11 after review. Pulmonary edema is the sign; acute heart failure driven by
a BP of 204/112 is the problem. CPAP is the key treatment, supported by moderate-quality
evidence (Cochrane review: fewer intubations and lower mortality in cardiogenic pulmonary
edema). Nitro 0.4 mg SL is offered to AEMT and PM with SBP >100, after asking about
PDE-5 drugs, with the protocol's limit of no more than a 20–25% drop in the first hour.
Withholding fluid is correct. BP in both arms is assessed, per VIII-A11. Rating albuterol
"not ideal" is reasonable: it doesn't treat the cause, and observational data link
beta-agonists to arrhythmia in heart failure.

### 3 · Passed Out at Home — ruptured ectopic
Sound. The risk factors (missed period, prior chlamydia), shoulder-tip pain and persistent
shock lying flat are all appropriate. Treating orthostatics in a patient already hypotensive
as a harm is correct. Rating oxygen "OK" at SpO2 98% follows VIII-A1; routine oxygen in
patients with normal saturation shows no benefit in other settings. Decisions 7 and 8
confirmed.

### 4 · Throat Feels Tight — anaphylaxis
Sound. The criteria are met (skin plus respiratory involvement). IM epinephrine first at every
level matches VIII-A3 and every major guideline, though the evidence is consensus and
observational, not randomized. The rebound at 15 minutes and repeat epinephrine fit "repeat
every 5–10 minutes." Antihistamines as add-ons only is correct (Cochrane: no randomized
evidence they treat anaphylaxis). Two protocol-level notes, outside the case itself:
- VIII-A3's PM IV epinephrine (0.1 mg 1:10,000 push) is a large bolus for a patient with a
  pulse. The game shows it as a poor first choice.
- VIII-A3 says biphasic reactions occur "in up to 20%". Pooled estimates are nearer 5%,
  though single studies range higher.

Decisions 5 and 6 applied.

### 5 · Just Heartburn — inferior STEMI with RV involvement
Sound, with the atropine fix above. The 12-lead (II, III, aVF elevation with reciprocal I/aVL)
and V4R are drawn correctly. Aspirin is strongly supported (ISIS-2). Withholding oxygen at
95% fits VIII-B2 and randomized trials (AVOID, DETO2X). The nitro hold rests on three
reasons: SBP under 100 (protocol threshold), tadalafil within 48 hours (strong
pharmacological basis), and RV involvement. The RV reason is the weakest: a 2016
observational study found nitro-related hypotension no more common in RV infarction. The
case is right to stack all three. Equal arm BPs are presented as making dissection less
likely, not excluding it, which is correct; pulse differentials are insensitive. AEMTs
may read V4R (decision 3). Open item: nitro scope, above.

### 6 · Just Weak — septic shock, urinary source
Sound, with the BP and wording fixes above. The metoprolol teaching point (HR 112 despite a
beta blocker) and the fluid-then-crackles stop are good. Fluid volumes match VIII-A12. The
game teaches qSOFA because the protocol uses it. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign (2021)
advises against qSOFA alone as a screen because it misses many septic patients. The review
note says a negative qSOFA doesn't exclude sepsis; consider saying that in the case too.
