Loading a set
Prime, load, latch, verify
  1. Prime a new set before loading it. Use only Pump Module / Gemini sets — another manufacturer's set will run inaccurately in this mechanism.
  2. Open the door. Hold the upper fitment above its recess and lower it in. Upper fitment goes in before the safety clamp fitment, and the tubing must not be twisted.
  3. Press the safety clamp fitment into the recess below the mechanism. When you reload a set, leave that fitment closed.
  4. With a fingertip, push the tubing firmly toward the back of the Air-in-Line detector. Tubing that is not fully seated is the usual cause of nuisance AIL alarms — and, per the current FDA notice, tubing must sit directly over the pressure sensors.
  5. Close the door and hold it shut with one hand on the door and case, then gently lower the latch. The safety clamp releases automatically as it latches. Do not touch the set while closing the door, and make sure no bedding, glove or other tubing is caught in it — that is a free-flow setup.
  6. Open the roller clamp.
  7. Verify no fluid is flowing in the drip chamber. Flow with the pump not running means free flow. Stop and fix it before the patient moves.

On a v9.0 PC Unit the safety clamp is labeled Flo-Stop. (DFU pp. 2-3 to 2-4.)

⚠
Free flow is a set-handling failure, not a pump failure. The pump cannot alarm for fluid running around it.

Before the patient moves, verify every line independently — not by repeating back what the sending nurse told you. This checklist saves on this device so you can work it at the bedside and clear it for the next call.

The commitment step
START is what changes the infusion. Typing a number changes nothing. Never assume a value took because you entered it — read it back off the display.
Change rate or VTBI on a running infusion
  1. Press CHANNEL SELECT on the correct module.
  2. Press the RATE or VTBI soft key.
  3. Enter the new value — up/down arrows to titrate, or the numeric keys.
  4. Re-read the whole program: drug, concentration, weight, dose, rate and VTBI.
  5. Press START to accept the change.
  6. Confirm the channel is infusing, not paused — green INFUSE light, expected drip-chamber flow, and the patient responding as you'd expect.

For a high-alert medication, get an independent double-check whenever operationally possible. (DFU pp. 2-57 to 2-58.)

Pause, restart, restore
Stopping temporarily

Press PAUSE to hold that channel. Resume with RESTART, or CHANNEL SELECT then START. A paused channel starts prompting visually and audibly after about two minutes — that prompt is the pump telling you a line is not running.

If an alert is already active, the PC Unit's PAUSE soft key will not take effect until the alert is addressed.

Do not hold CHANNEL OFF unless you mean to end that channel. Held until it beeps, it stops the infusion, deselects the module, and powers the system down if it was the only module running.

Restore: to restart with the parameters already stored, press the RESTORE soft key, verify what comes back, then press START. A bolus dose is stopped and restored through the Bolus Dose procedure, not these keys.

Guardrails does not verify the order
An accepted program is not a correct program

Soft limit: can be overridden by selecting Yes. The G, LLL or upward-arrow indicators mark an infusion running outside a soft limit — somebody chose to override it, and it may or may not have been the right call.

Hard limit: cannot be overridden. It has to be reprogrammed.

Basic / non-library infusion: may carry no dosing limits at all.

Wrong patient-care profile: gives you limits meant for a different population, and they will look just as authoritative.

Patient weight propagates. A weight entered on one module can carry to calculations on another, and changing it can recalculate dose or rate. Check every weight-based line after you touch a weight. (DFU pp. 2-27 to 2-30, 2-50, 2-84.)

Secondary (piggyback) delivery on this pump is partly gravity controlled. That is what makes it the most common thing to find set up wrong at a bedside — and the easiest thing to break by moving the patient.

The secondary must hang above the primary — roughly 9½ inches higher — and the primary line must contain a check valve. (DFU pp. 2-44 to 2-49.)
What has to be true for a secondary to run
  1. The primary line contains a check valve.
  2. The secondary clamp is open.
  3. The secondary bag hangs above the primary — about 9½ inches higher.
  4. The programmed secondary VTBI matches what is actually in the bag.

When the secondary VTBI reaches zero, the pump automatically returns to the primary program. Nothing about that transition depends on anyone noticing it.

The two ways it goes wrong
VTBI set too low
Medication left in the secondary bag runs at the primary rate
The pump has already switched back. The remaining antibiotic, or pressor, keeps draining at whatever the primary was programmed to run.
VTBI set too high
Primary fluid runs at the secondary rate
The secondary bag empties, the pump keeps counting down, and it pulls primary fluid at the secondary's rate — often far faster than intended.
⚠
Bag heights change every time you move the patient — loading, unloading, a stretcher raise, a pole knocked in a hallway. Recheck the hang after every move, not just at pickup.

Silencing an alarm suppresses the sound. It does not fix anything, and the condition is still there when the tone comes back. Tap an alarm for what it means and what to do.

⚠
Infiltration is not an alarm. The pump cannot detect it — it will keep pushing into the tissue at exactly the programmed rate. The only detector is you looking at the site and the patient. (DFU pp. 1-46 to 1-47, 2-81, 2-116 to 2-122.)
Long transports
Battery

The manual rates roughly six hours with one pump module, or about three hours with four — but only from a new, fully charged battery under the manufacturer's test conditions. A fleet battery in year four is not that battery.

Use the pump's own estimate: OPTIONS → PAGE DOWN → BATTERY RUNTIME, and run on external power whenever it is available.

Position and handling

The system must stay upright and securely mounted, with every module fully latched.

Low-rate occlusions alarm late. Even the older manual allows up to 45 minutes at 1 mL/h under one high-pressure setting before the occlusion alarm sounds. At low rates, your eyes on the drip chamber and the site beat the alarm every time.

Current FDA safety notices
⚠
Class I — infusion set performance (2025). BD's recall covers the Model 8100 Pump Module used with a subset of compatible pump infusion sets: real-world flow accuracy, occlusion-alarm delay and post-occlusion bolus can be worse than the user manual describes. Recalled July 2025, expanded in September 2025.
Mitigations while those sets are in service
  1. Keep the container between pump level and 21 inches above the top of the module — never below the pump, never higher than 21 inches.
  2. At rates below 10 mL/h, do not use Selectable pressure above 50 mmHg.
  3. Avoid flow rates below 1 mL/h.
  4. When loading, make sure the tubing lies directly over the pressure sensors.
  5. Confirm all clamps are open, no kinks or collapsed tubing outside the pump, the vent is open if the container has one, and drops are visibly flowing in the drip chamber.

AMR KC has to verify its own set catalog numbers and recall-correction status before these pumps go out on a truck. This page cannot tell you which sets in your kit are affected — that comes from fleet and clinical leadership against the current BD notice.

⚠
Class I — dropped or jarred modules (November 2025). A drop can damage the bezel assembly that holds the pumping mechanism in alignment. The damage is internal and not visible, and it can cause under-infusion, over-infusion, unregulated flow or a failure to calibrate. Two serious injuries were reported.
Non-negotiable
If a module is dropped or severely jarred, take it out of service immediately. A normal-looking display is not evidence it is safe. It goes back in use only after qualified service personnel test and inspect it.
ⓘ
The DFU this training is built on is the 2009 v9 manual. It is the right reference for how the keys work, and the wrong reference for current performance and recall status. Check both.
