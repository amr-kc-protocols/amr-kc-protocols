Facilitator Run-of-Show

A two-day, instructor-led plan for delivering all nine modules in person — with timed cues for exactly when to launch each quiz, drag-and-drop, branching scenario, and LTV 1200 simulator mission.

Jordan Hunter Jones, NRP · Course Director

How to run this course

  • Delivery model. You teach each module's lesson beats at the front (project the academy or your slides); learners then do that module's Knowledge Check, Match, Scenario, and Simulator on their own devices. This guide tells you when to hand off to each.
  • Where it lives. Learners open vta/academy.html; the simulator is vent-ltv1200.html. Every module's hub and its bottom nav row now carry a 🫁 Practice link straight to that module's simulator mission.
  • Modules lock in sequence. Module N stays locked until N−1 is fully complete — all lessons read, quiz passed (8/10), match perfect, scenario finished. Keep the cohort moving together so nobody is gated out.
  • Facilitator override. Open the academy with ?admin (e.g. academy.html?admin) to unlock every module and the final exam for demos and back-tracking. Footer Admin button toggles it off. Password-gated in the footer for shared devices.
  • Reset between cohorts. Progress is per-browser in localStorage. The footer Reset progress button clears a device for the next learner.
  • Pass marks. Each Knowledge Check: 8/10. Match: all correct. Final Certification Exam: 20/25 (80%), then learners claim a printable certificate.
  • Pre-flight. Confirm hosting URL on the room Wi-Fi, one charged device per learner (or pairs), audio on for the simulator alarms, and a projector for scenarios. Do a POST on the simulator once yourself before the room arrives.

Day 1

Module 1 · Pulmonary Anatomy & Physiology

The foundational hour. Learn how a healthy breath works — then use that to read every vent for the rest of the course.

67 min
teach 28 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 7 lessons (~28 min)

  1. 1.1 · Anatomy — The Path of a Breath. A breath begins at the nose or mouth and travels through the pharynx, larynx, and trachea. Pearl: ETT depth at the lip
  2. 1.2 · Function — Two Functional Zones. The pulmonary system has two zones. Evidence: Surfactant and PEEP
  3. 1.3 · The Mental Model — Ventilation vs Oxygenation — Two Knobs Each. This is the single most useful mental model in mechanical ventilation — memorize it now. Pearl: Two knobs each
  4. 1.4 · Dead Space — Air That Doesn't Help You. Every breath is like mailing a package down a long hallway to a room at the far end.
  5. 1.5 · V/Q Matching — Shunt vs Dead Space. Picture a bus line where the buses are air and the passengers are blood. Pearl: FiO₂ alone won't fix shunt
  6. 1.6 · Compliance vs Resistance — How the Lung Pushes Back. Imagine blowing up a balloon through a straw — two completely separate things can make it hard.
  7. 1.7 · The Bedside Test — PIP vs Pplat. Picture pushing water through a hose that has a narrow nozzle (the airways) and a balloon on the end (the alveoli). Pearl: PIP up, Pplat up?

Emphasize: ETT depth at the lip · Two knobs each · FiO₂ alone won't fix shunt · PIP up, Pplat up?

Facilitator tip. Anchor everything on the two-knob model (1.3). If a learner leaves with one thing, it's RR+Vt for ventilation, FiO₂+PEEP for oxygenation.

Module 2 · Recognizing Respiratory Failure

Call it before the patient calls it. Type I vs Type II, the SpO₂/PaO₂ cliff, capnography waveforms, and the pre-arrest signs everyone misses.

67 min
teach 28 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 7 lessons (~28 min)

  1. 2.1 · Definitions — What Respiratory Failure Means. Acute respiratory failure is defined by gas-exchange criteria. Pearl: ABGs in EMS
  2. 2.2 · The Oxyhemoglobin Curve — Why SpO₂ Lies — Until It Doesn't. Pulse oximetry is not linear — the SpO₂/PaO₂ relationship is an S-shaped curve, and that shape is a flat tabletop that ends at a cliff. Evidence: Hyperoxia after ROSC
  3. 2.3 · Type I — Hypoxic Failure at the Bedside. Hypoxic failure presents first as work-of-breathing changes.
  4. 2.4 · Type II — Hypercapnic Failure at the Bedside. Hypercapnic failure is more deceptive, because a tiring pump goes quiet, not loud. Pearl: Quiet chest in asthma
  5. 2.5 · Recognition — Early Signs, Late Signs, and the False Calm. Early signs of respiratory failure are predictable.
  6. 2.6 · Capnography — EtCO₂ — The Earliest Number You Have. EtCO₂ is the partial pressure of CO₂ measured at the end of exhalation.
  7. 2.7 · Waveforms — Four Capnography Patterns Every EMS Provider Must Recognize. Normal: a box-like waveform. Pearl: Shark fin = bronchospasm

Emphasize: ABGs in EMS · Quiet chest in asthma · Shark fin = bronchospasm

Facilitator tip. Draw the capnography waveforms (2.7) live at the board — shark-fin vs box — before you send them to the quiz.

Module 3 · Indications for Ventilatory Support

Pick the right level of support — first time. CPAP for CHF. BiPAP for COPD. Invasive for failure. The evidence behind each and the contraindications that must not be missed.

63 min
teach 24 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 6 lessons (~24 min)

  1. 3.1 · NIV Indications — When Non-Invasive Ventilation Is the Right Tool. Non-invasive ventilation (NIV) delivers positive-pressure ventilation through a tightly-fitting mask rather than an endotracheal tube. Evidence: Strong evidence
  2. 3.2 · The Two Flavors — CPAP vs BiPAP. Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) is one steady pressure the whole cycle long — a doorstop wedged under the airway, holding it open the same amount whether the patient breathes in or out.
  3. 3.3 · Titration — How to Make NIV Work — and Recognize When It Doesn't. NIV that is going to work shows signs of working within minutes. Pearl: Sit them up
  4. 3.4 · Contraindications — When NIV Is the WRONG Tool. ABSOLUTE contraindications must be respected. Pearl: Vomiting on NIV
  5. 3.5 · Invasive — Indications for Endotracheal Intubation. Move to invasive mechanical ventilation when:
  6. 3.6 · Pre-Oxygenation — Buying Yourself Eight Minutes. Pre-oxygenation before RSI is topping off the tank before a free-dive: you flush the nitrogen out of the lung's reservoir — the functional residual capacity (FRC) — and fill it with oxygen, so the patient can hold that breath through the apnea of intubation far longer before desaturating. Pearl: NoDESAT

Emphasize: Sit them up · Vomiting on NIV · NoDESAT

Module 4 · Vent Vocabulary & Initial Settings

The eight numbers every vented patient has. IBW math, the ARDSNet history, and how to defend any setting you turn.

63 min
teach 24 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 6 lessons (~24 min)

  1. 4.1 · Tidal Volume — Vt — How Much Air Per Breath. Tidal volume is the volume the ventilator delivers with each mandatory breath, measured in milliliters. Evidence: ARDSNet ARMA, NEJM 2000
  2. 4.2 · Rate — Respiratory Rate & Minute Ventilation. Respiratory rate is the number of mandatory breaths per minute the vent delivers.
  3. 4.3 · Oxygenation Knobs — FiO₂ and PEEP. FiO₂ is the fraction of oxygen in the inspired gas. Pearl: FiO₂ alone won't fix shunt
  4. 4.4 · Timing — I:E Ratio. I:E is the ratio of inspiratory time to expiratory time.
  5. 4.5 · Pressures — PIP, Pplat, and Driving Pressure. Peak Inspiratory Pressure (PIP): highest airway pressure during inspiration. Evidence: Amato 2015 driving pressure
  6. 4.6 · Defaults — IBW Math and Safe Initial Settings. Devine IBW formulas. Pearl: 5'10" rule of thumb

Emphasize: FiO₂ alone won't fix shunt · 5'10" rule of thumb

Facilitator tip. Work the IBW math (4.6) on the whiteboard with a real height — they'll need it in the scenario and simulator.

Module 5 · Modes of Ventilation

Volume targeted, pressure targeted, and the partnerships in between. Pick the mode the patient needs — not the mode you remember.

63 min
teach 24 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 6 lessons (~24 min)

  1. 5.1 · Targeting — Volume vs Pressure Targeting. Every vent breath guarantees one thing and lets the other float — like inflating a tire: pump to a set volume of air and the pressure lands where it lands, or pump to a set pressure and the volume lands where it lands.
  2. 5.2 · Assist-Control — What "Control" Really Means. "Control" does NOT mean the patient is locked out. Pearl: Pure AC = full breath every time
  3. 5.3 · SIMV & Pressure Support — SIMV and Why It Fell Out of Favor. Synchronized Intermittent Mandatory Ventilation: vent delivers a SET rate of mandatory breaths. Evidence: Esteban 1995
  4. 5.4 · NIV Modes — CPAP vs BiPAP. CPAP: ONE pressure throughout the breath.
  5. 5.5 · PRVC — PRVC and Newer Modes. Pressure-Regulated Volume Control: a HYBRID.
  6. 5.6 · Synchrony — Trigger Sensitivity & Inspiratory Rise Time. Patient-vent dyssynchrony is the patient and the machine fighting over who leads the breath — pushing and pulling at different moments, like two people trying to row out of step.

Emphasize: Pure AC = full breath every time

Facilitator tip. Stress that the LTV has no PRVC — the M5 scenario turns on substituting AC/VC. Preview it before the sim.

Module 7 · Alarms & Troubleshooting

Every alarm is information. DOPE is the universal algorithm. The 10-second rule is non-negotiable.

67 min
teach 28 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 7 lessons (~28 min)

  1. 7.1 · Philosophy — What Alarms Are For. Alarms are NOT nuisances. Pearl: Sentinel error
  2. 7.2 · D — Dislodgement. Most common cause of sudden vent crisis in transport.
  3. 7.3 · O — Obstruction. Tube patent in position but blocked.
  4. 7.4 · P — Pneumothorax. Sudden rise in PIP, often with falling SpO₂ and BP.
  5. 7.5 · E — Equipment Failure. Machine is the problem.
  6. 7.6 · The 10-Second Rule — When to Disconnect and Bag. If you cannot identify and fix an alarm in 10 seconds, disconnect from the ventilator and bag with 100% oxygen. Pearl: 10 seconds, then bag
  7. 7.7 · Dyssynchrony — When the Patient and Vent Argue. Patient-vent dyssynchrony affects ~25% of vented patients (Blanch, ICM 2015).

Emphasize: Sentinel error · 10 seconds, then bag

Facilitator tip. Drill the 10-second rule (7.6) as a live call-and-response before the DOPE scenario.

Module 8 · Sedation & Analgesia

If the patient can fight the vent, the vent will lose. Analgesia first. Push-dose pressors ready before you push the induction agent.

59 min
teach 20 · quiz 10 · match 5 · scenario 12 · sim 12

Teach — 5 lessons (~20 min)

  1. 8.1 · Why Sedate — Five Reasons. Comfort: an ETT is uncomfortable; bucking the vent is exhausting. Pearl: Iron rule
  2. 8.2 · RASS — The Sedation Scale. Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale runs +4 (combative) to −5 (unarousable).
  3. 8.3 · Agents — EMS Sedation/Analgesia Toolbox. Fentanyl 1–2 mcg/kg IV, redose 0.5–1 mcg/kg q15–20 min.
  4. 8.4 · Push-Dose Pressors — Ready BEFORE You Push Induction. Post-intubation hypotension affects ~25% of EMS RSI (Heffner 2012). Evidence: Post-intubation hypotension
  5. 8.5 · Pitfalls — What Goes Wrong. Under-sedation in the first 10 minutes after intubation.

Emphasize: Iron rule

Facilitator tip. Analgesia-first and 'awareness with paralysis' are the safety messages — hammer 8.1 and 8.5.

Same vent. Different goals. Asthma/COPD, ARDS, TBI, pediatrics. Pick the goal first; the settings follow.

  5. 9.5 · TBI — Strict Normocapnia. Two enemies of the injured brain: hypoxia and hypotension. Pearl: Hyperventilation past the rescue window is harmful
